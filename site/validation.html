Reference-solver comparison — OpenFOAM v2606, same mesh, same ν

icoFoam configured for maximum fidelity: pure central interpolation (Gauss linear), 2nd-order backward time, PISO with 2 correctors, Co ≈ 0.2, uniform orthogonal hex mesh identical to ours. No turbulence model in either code — both runs are "DNS setups"; the difference is the discretization. Thin dashed line: the part of OpenFOAM's dissipation actually resolved by viscosity, ν⟨|∇u|²⟩.

Reading — at 128³ OpenFOAM's total dissipation looks respectable, but roughly half of it is produced by the scheme (Rhie–Chow flux interpolation + PISO splitting), not by viscosity: the energy leaves through numerics, implicit-LES style. At 64³ this dominates and the peak arrives 3.4 time units early. Our staggered energy-conserving scheme keeps the numerical share at 0.3–0.4% on the same mesh, at ~3000× lower wall-clock per step. Scope fairly: OpenFOAM buys general geometry with that dissipation; this solver only does periodic boxes — which is exactly what DNS of homogeneous turbulence needs.
